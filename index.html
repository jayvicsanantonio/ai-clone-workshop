Before You Begin

This workshop is designed so you do one session per weekday (5 per week) for 6 weeks. Each session takes 30–60 minutes. By the end, you'll have a fully personalized AI system built on Claude that handles real work.

You don't need any coding experience. You don't need any prior AI experience. You just need Claude and the willingness to actually do the exercises — not just read them.

Clone Builder — Build It In One Week

Day 1 The Brain Dump — Map Your World +

What You'll Do

Today is about extracting everything from your head and putting it into a format Claude can work with. You're going to create your personal context document.

Step 1 — Write Your "About Me" Brief (15 min)

Open your AI Clone Playbook doc and write answers to these questions. Be specific — generic answers create a generic clone.

  1. What do you do for work? (Title, industry, company type, who you serve)
  2. What are your 3 biggest professional goals for the next 6 months?
  3. What are your 3 biggest personal goals?
  4. What does a typical work week look like? (Walk through Monday to Friday)
  5. What tools/platforms do you use daily? (Email, Slack, Notion, etc.)
  6. What are the recurring tasks you do every week?
  7. What are the tasks you hate doing but can't avoid?
  8. What's your communication style? (Formal? Casual? Direct? Warm?)

Step 2 — Feed It to Claude (15 min)

Now take everything you wrote and bring it to Claude with this prompt:

I'm building a personal AI Clone system with you — an AI partner trained on my context that can help me with daily work. Here's my full context. I want you to read this carefully, then do two things: 1. Summarize back to me who I am and what I need in a structured "Clone Profile" format 2. Ask me 10 follow-up questions that would help you assist me even better — things I probably forgot to mention Here's my info: [PASTE YOUR ABOUT ME BRIEF]

Step 3 — Refine the Profile (15 min)

Answer Claude's follow-up questions. Then ask Claude to produce a final "Clone Profile v1" that combines everything. Save this to your Playbook doc — this is the foundation of everything.

Pro tip: Set this Clone Profile as your Claude "User Preferences" in Settings. That way every new conversation starts with your context already loaded.
Day 2 The Time Audit — Find Your 15 Hours +

What You'll Do

You can't buy back time you haven't accounted for. Today you audit every recurring task and categorize it by AI-readiness.

Step 1 — Task Inventory (20 min)

I want to audit my time to find 15+ hours a week I can buy back with AI. Here's what I need you to do: 1. Based on my Clone Profile, list every recurring task you think I do weekly (professional and personal). Make educated guesses — I'll correct you. 2. For each task, estimate the time per week. 3. Categorize each task into one of 4 buckets: - 🟢 AUTOMATE NOW: Claude can do 80%+ of this right away - 🟡 ACCELERATE: Claude can cut the time in half with the right setup - 🟠 AUGMENT: Claude can help but I still drive - 🔴 HUMAN ONLY: Requires my physical presence or deeply personal judgment Format this as a table with columns: Task | Time/Week | Category | How Claude Helps

Step 2 — Correct and Complete (15 min)

Review Claude's list. Add tasks it missed. Correct time estimates. Push back where you disagree on categories — this conversation will teach Claude how you think about your work.

Step 3 — Build Your Priority Queue (10 min)

Now sort all the 🟢 and 🟡 tasks by this formula: (time saved per week) × (ease of setup with Claude). Give me a ranked priority list of the top 10 tasks to automate/accelerate first, with a one-line description of how we'll set each one up.

Save this priority list. It's your roadmap for the next 5 weeks.

Day 3 Style & Voice Calibration +

What You'll Do

The difference between a useful AI and one that feels "off" is voice. Today you teach Claude exactly how you communicate.

Step 1 — Collect Writing Samples (15 min)

Gather 5–10 real examples of things you've written. The more variety, the better:

  • 2–3 emails you've sent (work emails, client emails, casual emails)
  • 1–2 social media posts or messages
  • 1 longer piece (blog post, report, proposal section)
  • Any Slack/Teams messages that feel very "you"

Step 2 — Voice Analysis

I'm going to give you samples of my real writing. I want you to analyze my communication style deeply and create a "Voice Profile" I can use in future prompts. Focus on: - Tone (formal ↔ casual spectrum) - Sentence structure (short & punchy vs. long & flowing) - Vocabulary level and any signature phrases I use - How I open and close messages - My personality that comes through (humor? directness? warmth?) - Any quirks or patterns Here are my writing samples: [PASTE YOUR SAMPLES] After analyzing, create a "Voice Profile" document I can paste into future prompts so Claude writes like me.

Step 3 — Test It

Now test the voice profile immediately:

Using my Voice Profile above, draft a short email declining a meeting invitation because I have a scheduling conflict. Keep it 100% in my voice.

Compare Claude's draft to how you'd actually write it. Give it feedback: "Too formal," "I'd never say 'I appreciate your understanding,'" "Add a bit more warmth." Iterate until it nails your voice. Save the final Voice Profile to your Playbook.

Pro tip: You can use Claude's custom Style feature. Go to Settings → Style → Create a custom style. Paste your writing samples and Claude will learn your voice across all conversations.

Day 23 Advanced Prompting Techniques +

What You'll Do

Level up your prompting with advanced techniques that get dramatically better results.

Technique 1: Chain of Thought

Add "Think step by step before answering" to analytical prompts. This dramatically improves accuracy on complex problems.

Technique 2: Role + Anti-Role

You are an experienced [ROLE] who specializes in [SPECIALTY]. You are NOT a generic AI assistant — you have strong opinions backed by deep expertise. Don't hedge or give wishy-washy answers. Tell me what you actually think is the right move and why.

Technique 3: Few-Shot Examples

Always provide 1-2 examples of ideal output when quality matters. Claude mimics patterns extremely well.

Technique 4: Structured Output Formats

Always specify exactly how you want the output formatted: "Use headers, bullet points, and bold key terms" or "Write in prose paragraphs, no bullets" or "Format as a table with columns for X, Y, Z."

Technique 5: Iterative Refinement

Instead of trying to get the perfect output in one prompt, use a sequence: "Draft it → Now make it shorter → Now make the opening stronger → Now add a specific example in paragraph 2."

Practice

Take your 3 weakest-performing prompts and apply these techniques. See if the output improves.
